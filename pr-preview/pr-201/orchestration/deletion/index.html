Orchestration/Deletion and holdsIn development

Deletion and holds

Units leave an environment when source stops declaring them, when the Environment no longer selects them, or when an operator tears them down. Nyl never destroys anything by accident: a unit that leaves the ownership set waits for an explicit --allow-teardown, units are torn down consumers first, and a unit that should keep its resources leaves a tombstone instead. This page explains how a run deletes units, how nyl teardown tears them down on request, and how nyl hold freezes a unit during an incident.

The lifecycle of a unit

Section titled “The lifecycle of a unit”

Every desired document in state has a lifecycle block. A unit’s whole history is the diff of one file, and the last desired document a teardown needs stays in it:

nyl status shows the states as pending-teardown (deleting, teardown not started), tearing-down (the driver’s teardown started, such as a publication waiting under its teardownWait), held, and retained.

Leaving the ownership set

Section titled “Leaving the ownership set”

A unit leaves the ownership set when it is removed from source, deselected by the Environment’s unitSelector, or disabled with enabled: false. What happens next depends on its deletionPolicy:

PolicyDefault forWhen it leaves
TeardownKinds that can tear down, such as a Command with a teardown stepIt becomes pending-teardown; nyl reconcile --allow-teardown tears it down and removes its files.
Retain, declaredUnits that declare itIt becomes a retained tombstone: its resources stay, its last desired document, uid, and receipt are kept in state.
Retain, by kindKinds that cannot tear down, such as a Command without a teardown stepIt is released: its resources stay in place and its files are removed. The transition commit lists what was left.

A teardown caused by a change to source needs --allow-teardown. Without it the unit stays pending-teardown, keeping its uid and receipt, and the run exits 2 with NYL-TEARDOWN-PENDING. A selector typo therefore destroys nothing, and fixing it returns the unit to active with its receipt, so it does not run again. nyl plan lists leaving units first, with the destroy plan --allow-teardown would carry out.

Here cache (a Command with a teardown step) and worker (which reads cache’s endpoint and declares deletionPolicy: Retain) were removed from source:

--allow-teardown gates only the start of a teardown. A teardown that started and failed, or that waits, continues in the next run without it, and so does a teardown an operator requested with nyl teardown.

nyl reconcile --leaving acts only on the units outside the ownership set; nyl reconcile --unit does no deletion work at all.

Order

Section titled “Order”

Teardown follows the reverse of creation order: a unit is torn down only after every deleting unit that depends on it is gone, so consumers go first and producers follow once nothing uses them. Retentions and releases follow the same rule, so a producer’s tombstone is written only once its deleting consumers are torn down. A unit with no dependency path to a unit that waits is torn down in the same run.

Retained tombstones

Section titled “Retained tombstones”

A retained tombstone keeps running resources that may still use their producers. Tearing down a unit that a tombstone’s receipt cites refuses with NYL-TEARDOWN-HAS-DEPENDENTS (exit 2) unless the invocation passes --allow-dependents; the transition commit records the override under allowDependents.

worker’s tombstone cites cache, so tearing cache down needs both overrides:

A unit that returns with the same name and kind adopts its tombstone. Its execution key alone decides: when the key is unchanged, the tombstone’s receipt is current at once and nothing runs again, even when a producer was recreated with a new uid in the meantime. Otherwise the unit runs as an ordinary change of the same incarnation. A unit that must run again whenever its producer is recreated says so in its inputs, for example by consuming an output that changes with every creation.

Restoring both units afterwards creates a new cache with the same endpoint, and worker adopts its tombstone without running:

nyl state forget drops a tombstone or a pending-teardown unit from state without touching its resources.

Kind changes

Section titled “Kind changes”

Changing a unit’s apiVersion or kind under the same name ends the old incarnation: it becomes deleting with reason: kind-change, which needs --allow-teardown like any omission, and the run warns with NYL-UNIT-KIND-CHANGE. The new kind waits until the old incarnation’s file is removed, then starts with a new uid; a deleting incarnation of any kind fences a new one the same way. A retained tombstone of another kind is dropped with its resources left in place.

Explicit teardown

Section titled “Explicit teardown”

nyl teardown -e <env> --unit <u> tears a unit down whether or not it is in the ownership set. The command is explicit intent, so it needs no --allow-teardown. It acts on the documents in state and renders nothing: each teardown runs with the files of the unit’s recorded sourceRevision. When that commit can no longer be fetched, the teardown fails before any effect with a fix naming --source <revision>, which runs it with another revision’s files and records the override in the transition commit.

  • A unit outside the ownership set (a deleting unit, or a tombstone) is torn down and its files are removed.
  • A unit still in the ownership set is replaced (reason: replace): its incarnation is torn down, and the next reconcile creates a new one with a new uid. Dependents resolve against the new receipt’s outputs and run only when their execution key changes.
  • --hold holds the unit in the same operation, so no run recreates it between the teardown and a separate nyl hold: its desired file stays, held, without a uid.
  • --all tears down every unit, dependents first, and holds the units of the ownership set afterwards. It honours deletionPolicy: Retain: declared Retain units, retained tombstones, and kinds that cannot tear down are released, keeping their resources, and listed under released in the transition commit.

A teardown that fails leaves the teardown in progress with a failed condition (exit 3), and one whose outcome is unknown, or that passes the unit’s timeout, leaves it uncertain (exit 4). The kind’s teardown recovery policy decides what happens next, as for executions: an idempotent teardown runs again in the next run, and anything else waits for nyl recover.

Holds

Section titled “Holds”

nyl hold -e <env> --unit <u> makes runs change nothing about a unit, whatever state it is in, without touching its resources. A held unit never progresses:

  • A running incarnation keeps the desired document its receipt was produced for, so its consumers keep resolving against what actually runs. Runs record what source would change under hold.pending, and write the held file only when that changes.
  • A unit without an incarnation, including one that was never reconciled in the environment, is not created; its dependents stay blocked.
  • A deleting unit pauses where it is.
  • A teardown of a held unit only records the request on the held file and tears nothing down; the run reports the unit held and exits 0. After nyl resume, the next reconcile or teardown carries the request out.
  • A held unit that leaves the ownership set is reported as held with a pending deletion. After nyl resume, a held file without an incarnation outside the ownership set is removed by the next reconcile.

nyl hold also works while a run holds the environment’s lease: the run reads the hold before it starts each unit, starts no held unit, and writes the hold in its own transition commit. Holds are checked between teardown batches. Holding a publication during its teardown wait pauses it before phase two; its recorded wait stays in state until you resume it.

Publication teardown waits

Section titled “Publication teardown waits”

A KubernetesPublication unit tears down in two phases: it publishes the removal, waits under its teardownWait until Argo CD has removed the workloads, and only then removes the rest. While it waits, the units it depends on wait with it, reported with NYL-TEARDOWN-WAITING, and units with no path to it proceed. Before a publication’s teardown starts, Nyl checks that its target’s settings let the teardown complete; an unmet requirement keeps the unit pending-teardown unless the invocation passes --allow-incomplete, and a teardown completed without a claim of removal is recorded under incomplete with NYL-TEARDOWN-INCOMPLETE.

Codes

Section titled “Codes”
CodeMeaning and fix
NYL-TEARDOWN-PENDINGA teardown waits: for --allow-teardown, for the deleting units that depend on it, or for an approval. Pass --allow-teardown, restore the unit in source, or drop it with nyl state forget.
NYL-TEARDOWN-HAS-DEPENDENTSRetained tombstones cite the unit. Pass --allow-dependents, or forget the tombstones first.
NYL-TEARDOWN-WAITINGA publication it depends on waits under its teardownWait. Run again once the wait is over, or confirm the removal with nyl teardown --confirm-removed.
NYL-TEARDOWN-INCOMPLETEThe teardown cannot complete, or completed without a claim of removal. Meet the requirements, or pass --allow-incomplete and check what remains.
NYL-UNIT-RELEASEDA unit’s resources were left in place. Remove them by hand when nothing needs them.
NYL-UNIT-KIND-CHANGEA unit changed its kind; the old incarnation is deleting. Pass --allow-teardown, restore the old kind, or forget the old incarnation.
NYL-UNIT-HELDA held unit keeps changes or a teardown from applying. Lift the hold with nyl resume.
